# FINDINGS — flagged bit flips in Elevator-code memories

What this work establishes, each claim with its evidence.  Numbers come from
`results/summary/numbers.json` and the tables in `results/summary/` (regenerated by
`./reproduce.sh analysis`; every raw result is in `results/`).  Methods: `docs/methods.md`.

Conventions: logical error rates are per inner repetition-code round and per logical qubit;
overhead = physical qubits (ancillas included) per logical qubit = n_b (2 d_Z − 1) / k; the
main target is p_Z = 1e-3, noise bias η = 1e6 (p_X = 1e-9), 1e-12 per round.

## 1. The published results are reproduced once two unstated circuit details are fixed

1. **The published Figures 1 and 2 follow exactly from the paper's own fitted models.**
   Recomputed overheads: 187 → 93.5 → 88 → 58.7 with steps at η = 6.7e4, 1.21e5 and 1.76e6
   (Fig. 1), thin surface code 229 → 125 at η = 1.03e5, XZZX 145; at p_Z = 1e-2 the floors are
   1.94e-9 ([15,9,3], d_Z = 39) and 2.08e-11 ([15,6,5] with two ancillas, d_Z = 49).
   Evidence: `results/summary/fig1_from_paper_fits.png`, `fig2_from_paper_fits.png`,
   `numbers.json:fig1_elevator_steps`, `fig2_from_fits_floors`; test `tests/test_core.py`.

2. **The paper's circuits evidently carry no idle noise on blocks that are not taking part in a
   logical-operation tick.**  With idle noise on every waiting block (the literal reading of
   Table I) the simulated rates are 3–9× above the published fits for both memories
   (Z memory: 0/16, 3/16 and 1/16 points within 2×; X memory: 7.5–8.5× at d_Z = 9); without it
   (“noop”) [15,6,5] agrees at 15/16 points (ratios 0.43–1.33), [15,6,5] with two ancillas at
   14/16 (0.60–2.45), and the X memory at 0.44–0.55×.  The same choice makes the isolated
   repetition code (same inner round) agree with the paper's repetition-code fit within
   0.7–1.4× over p_Z = 1e-3…1e-2, d_Z = 5…21.  Evidence: `results/summary/reproduction.md`,
   `results/summary/phase_model.md`.

3. **The ancilla path is not stated; a full sweep per check, alternating down and up, is the
   reading consistent with the paper's statements** (n_L = 15 < d_Z in the operating regime,
   m d_Z rounds per outer round there).  A 'compressed' variant with exactly d_Z rounds per check
   changes rates by < 2× and agrees no better; the check order changes [15,9,3] by ≤ 15 % and the
   number of outer rounds by ≤ 10 %.  Evidence: `reproduction.md`, `results/sensitivity_15_9_3.json`
   (`scripts/sensitivity_15_9_3.py`).

4. **One residual disagreement: [15,9,3] bit flips are 1.4–3.8× above the published fit at
   p_X ≤ 2e-6** (within 2× for p_X ≥ 4.5e-6, 6/16 points within 2× overall).  It is not the
   decoder (exact maximum likelihood still gives 1.9–2.5× at p_X = 1–2e-6), not the schedule
   variant, idle reading, check order or number of outer rounds (above; `results/sensitivity_15_9_3.json`).  Our [15,9,3]
   bit-flip baseline is therefore pessimistic relative to the paper by up to ~3×; every flagged
   result below is compared with our own flag-free baseline as well as with the published one.

## 2. Decoding with flags is optimal at the leading order

5. **Exact most-likely-error decoding (integer program) equals exact maximum likelihood on the
   configurations that dominate failure.**  On 3000 sampled leading-order configurations of
   [15,9,3] (d_Z = 17) with and without exactly timed flags, the two decoders fail on the same
   number of shots within statistics (72/72, 69/68, 15/17, 0/0, 72/72, 8/8; ≥ 99 % of decisions
   identical); BP+OSD fails 20–30 % more where failures occur.  Evidence:
   `results/decoder_optimality.json`, `scripts/decoder_optimality.py`.

6. **Known answers are met exactly.**  Repetition codes of distance 3, 5, 7, 9 with every flip
   flagged correct every pattern of up to d − 1 flips (all patterns enumerated) and fail on
   exactly half the flip patterns when all d bits are erased.  [15,9,3] and [15,6,5] with perfect
   flags and no other noise correct every pattern of up to d − 1 erased blocks — at code capacity
   (all 451 and 25 931 flip patterns) and in the full circuit (all block subsets at three times:
   1 353 and 77 793 patterns) — and fail only on erasure sets that contain a codeword (9 and 12
   of them).  Evidence: `results/known_answers.json`, `scripts/known_answers.py`.

## 3. Phase flips: the floor that flags cannot lower

7. **Phase flips of the elevator memory are set by its data blocks, which behave like isolated
   repetition codes, plus its logical ancilla, which behaves like a repetition code at 1.9× the
   noise.**  Switching off the noise of the logical ancilla and of the logical-operation CNOTs
   makes the elevator's phase-flip rate equal to (n_b/k) × the isolated repetition code
   (ratio 0.65–1.23); with it on the ratio grows with d_Z (1.1–1.5 at d_Z = 9, 1.8 at 13, 4.5 at
   17, ~10 at 21, p_Z = 1e-2).  Fitted model: p_ZL k = n a p_rep(d, p) + n_anc g p_rep(d, κp)
   with a = 1.13, g = 0.12, κ = 1.88.  This is the paper's lower elevator threshold (1/34.4 versus
   1/25 for the repetition code) made explicit: the ancilla takes part in every logical
   operation.  Evidence: `results/summary/phase_model.md` (ancilla diagnostic table).

8. **The repetition-code model predicts held-out points.**  Leaving out the lowest-rate point at
   each p_Z and refitting predicts it within 1.05–1.63× (all inside the 95 % intervals), down to
   1.3e-9 per round.  Evidence: `phase_model.md`, `numbers.json:phase_rep_fit.heldout`.
